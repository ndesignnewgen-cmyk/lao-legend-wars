LAO LEGEND WARS · FESTIVAL ART (12 images)
During a match a Lao festival comes round every 3 rounds and changes a rule. Each festival gets a wide banner (shown big when the festival
starts) and a round badge (shown on the board while it lasts).

HOW TO MAKE THEM
Please create these images one at a time, in order, numbered 1–12. After each image, continue with the next one without asking me.
Save each image as its number only (1.png … 12.png). No text, no letters, no numbers, no logos, no watermark in any picture.
All-ages, joyful and respectful: real Lao festivals, Lao faces and clothes (sinh, pha biang, salong), Lao temples with tiered roofs and naga
finials. Monks may appear only small and in the background, shown respectfully.

BANNERS (1–6) · STYLE: wide festival key art in the same look as the game's cards: Southeast Asian comic style, bold clean outlines, rich
warm colors, glowing lights, lots of joyful detail. Landscape 1600x900 (16:9). Keep the CENTER of the picture calm and a little darker (soft
sky or water), because the festival's name and rule will be written over the middle by the game; put the people and the brightest details
toward the left, right and bottom edges.

1. Bun Pi Mai (Lao New Year, April): people splashing water and blessing each other with flower water in silver bowls, sand stupas with
   little flags, frangipani petals, the guardian lions Sing Kaew and Sing Kham dancing, Luang Prabang temple roofs, sunny turquoise sky.
2. Bun Bang Fai (rocket festival): big decorated bamboo rockets with naga heads launching from wooden ramps, smoke trails and fireworks,
   villagers cheering with drums and khaen, green rice fields and hills, bright blue sky with a rainbow.
3. Bun Suang Heua (boat racing): long naga-headed race boats with rows of rowers racing on the Mekong, splashes, crowds and colorful
   umbrellas on the bank, drums, flags, the That Luang–style golden stupa far away, late afternoon gold light.
4. Bun That Luang (November, Vientiane): the great golden stupa Pha That Luang glowing at night, a procession with flower offering cones
   (pha sat) and candles, lanterns, families in festive clothes, stars, warm golden light.
5. Bun Ok Phansa and Lai Heua Fai (end of Buddhist Lent): glowing boats of fire made of banana trunks and lights floating on the river at
   night, little lotus lanterns on the water, fireworks, families on the riverbank, reflections everywhere.
6. Bun Khao Salak / Khao Sak (offering festival): families preparing small baskets of sticky rice, fruit and sweets wrapped in banana
   leaves, offering trays at a temple at dawn, soft candle light, flowers, peaceful and warm, a few novice monks small in the background.

BADGES (7–12) · STYLE: match the game's menu icons exactly: a glossy round game badge with a thick ornate golden Lao kranok frame, jewels
(rubies, sapphires), soft bevel and shine, one clear symbol in the middle, strong silhouette that reads at 40 pixels. Square 1024x1024,
on a PLAIN PURE WHITE background, no shadow on the ground (the game cuts the badge out).

7. Pi Mai badge: a silver water bowl with frangipani flowers and splashing water drops, a small sand stupa with a flag.
8. Bang Fai badge: a golden bamboo rocket with a naga head shooting upward on a trail of sparks.
9. Suang Heua badge: a golden naga-headed race boat with crossed paddles over blue waves.
10. That Luang badge: the golden stupa Pha That Luang with a soft halo.
11. Ok Phansa badge: a glowing lotus lantern boat on dark water with a small flame.
12. Khao Sak badge: a small woven basket of sticky rice with a banana leaf parcel and a candle.

How the game will use them: 1–6 the big festival announcement and the board's background tint for that festival · 7–12 the festival badge
on the board and in the festival list.
