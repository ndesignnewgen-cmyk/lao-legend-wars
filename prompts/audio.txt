LAO LEGEND WARS · MUSIC AND SOUND (7 music tracks + 26 sound effects)
A Lao-folklore card game for children. The game switches music by itself as a match goes (normal → about to win / about to lose)
and plays the sound effects on cards, attacks, rituals and legends.
Name each file exactly as below (any format is fine: mp3, wav or m4a). Send two zips: music.zip and sfx.zip.
You do not need to match the volumes: the game evens them out. Make two or three takes of anything you are unsure of and send them all (name them battle-a, battle-b …).

ALL MUSIC
· Instrumental only. NO vocals, NO singing, NO humming, NO choir words, NO spoken words.
· Lao sound: khaen (bamboo mouth organ, the drone and chords), phin (plucked Lao lute, the melody), khong wong / pong lang (wooden xylophone),
  ching (small finger cymbals keeping time), klong (barrel drum), a large khong (gong) for big moments. A light orchestral bed (strings, soft brass) is welcome under them.
· Scale: A minor pentatonic (A C D E G), like the khaen's "lai yai" mode. Keep every track in this key so the game can blend one into another.
· Family-friendly and adventurous, like a cartoon adventure film. Never scary, never horror, no harsh distortion, no sudden very loud hits.
· The loop tracks (1, 3, 4, 5) must keep the same feel from start to end: no intro build, no fade-out, no ending, no big break in the middle.
  At least 90 seconds each. I cut the seamless loop myself on bar lines, so steady tempo matters more than anything else.

--- MUSIC ---
1. menu (loop, 76 BPM, A minor pentatonic, 90–120 s)
   Calm, warm Lao village morning. Soft khaen chords breathing slowly, a gentle phin melody, light pong lang ripples, ching on every other beat, no heavy drums.
   Peaceful and inviting, for browsing cards and the shop. Instrumental, no vocals.
2. start (one-shot, 100 BPM, A minor pentatonic, 6–8 s)
   The opening of a match: a big khong gong, then klong drums rolling up and a bright khaen fanfare, ending on a strong held chord
   that leads straight into track 3 at 100 BPM. Exciting, heroic, no vocals.
3. battle (loop, 100 BPM, A minor pentatonic, 90–150 s)
   The normal battle. Energetic and playful, steady klong drum groove, ching on the off-beats, phin and khong wong trading a catchy melody,
   khaen chords pushing forward, light strings underneath. Exciting but not stressful. Instrumental, no vocals.
4. winning (loop, 100 BPM, A minor pentatonic, 90–120 s)
   The same battle feel when the player is about to win: brighter and more triumphant, fuller drums, heroic khaen and soft brass playing a rising melody,
   a gong accent every 8 bars. Must sit at exactly 100 BPM in A minor pentatonic so it can crossfade with track 3. Instrumental, no vocals.
5. losing (loop, 100 BPM, A minor pentatonic, 90–120 s)
   The same battle feel when the player is in danger: tense and suspenseful but still brave, lower khaen drone, a pulsing low klong heartbeat,
   a worried phin melody in the low register, quiet tremolo strings. Exciting, "you can still turn this around" — never sad, never scary.
   Exactly 100 BPM in A minor pentatonic so it can crossfade with track 3. Instrumental, no vocals.
6. victory (one-shot, 6–9 s)
   Celebration fanfare after winning: a joyful khaen and brass phrase, festive klong and ching, a bright gong at the end, ringing out naturally.
   Proud and happy, like a Lao festival. Instrumental, no vocals.
7. defeat (one-shot, 5–7 s)
   Gentle phrase after losing: soft phin and khaen, slow and warm, ending on a hopeful chord that says "try again". Kind, not sad, not dramatic. Instrumental, no vocals.

· Length as given; shorter is better than longer.
